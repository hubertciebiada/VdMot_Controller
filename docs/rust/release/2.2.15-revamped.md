## Highlights

Changes since 2.2.14. The full list is at the end of these notes.

- **A curve of up to 32 points.** `points` of `/api/valves/{n}/flow` takes 2 to 32 pairs, 2 to 16
  before. A flow equal to the flow of a point maps to exactly that point's position, so a curve
  with a point at every target the regulator sets, for example every 0.1 l/min from 0.5 to
  2.7 l/min with `[0,0]` before them (24 points), needs no interpolation for those targets.
- **A flow below the first flow of the curve is that point.** A target above 0 and below the first
  flow above 0 of the curve goes to the point of that flow and answers `clipped:true`; before, it
  lay on the line from the point without flow. With `[0,0]` and `[1070,0.5]` a target of
  0.3 l/min is 1070 pulses, no longer 642. A target of 0 still closes, and with R2 the floor
  `floorCounts` + 60 still raises a position below it.
- **A downgrade runs 16 of the points; 2.2.15 gets them all back.** NVS `flow` keeps the format of
  2.2.14. Of a curve of more than 16 points it holds 16, the first, the last and 14 evenly spaced
  by index, and the new key `flowpts` holds all of them. 2.2.14 and 2.2.13 ignore `flowpts` and run
  such a curve with those 16 points. For 24 points from 0.5 to 2.7 l/min after `[0,0]` they are
  0, 0.6, 0.7, 0.9, 1.0, 1.2, 1.3, 1.5, 1.6, 1.8, 1.9, 2.1, 2.2, 2.4, 2.5 and 2.7 l/min: the first
  point of the table, 0.5 l/min, is not among them, and since 2.2.14 has no rule for a flow below
  the first flow, 0.5 l/min there lies on the line from `[0,0]` to the point of 0.6 l/min. With
  no curve above 16 points `flowpts` is removed, and NVS is what 2.2.14 writes.
- **A failed save drops no points.** A save writes `flowpts` before `flow`. After a `500 nvs`
  the next `POST`, also one without `points`, saves a whole curve: the one the valve had, or the
  new one where NVS already holds all of it.
- **A cut curve shows.** A valve that may run 16 points of a longer curve, because `flowpts` is
  bad or does not match `flow` (a power loss between the two writes, a save of 2.2.14), gets event
  419 `flow_curve_cut` at the load and `"curveCut":true` in `GET /api/valves/{n}/flow` until a
  `POST` sets its points again. A `POST` without `points` saves those 16 points as the curve, and
  the mark then lasts until the next restart.
- **MQTT and Home Assistant** do not change. `tools/flowbench/flowcheck.py` keeps up to 32 points
  of a curve, 16 before.
- **STM32.** The code is that of 2.2.9; only the version number changes. Flashing the STM is
  optional: it keeps the versions of the two MCUs equal. Coming from 2.2.7 or older, flash it as
  2.2.13 says.

## Known limits

- **2.2.15 ran on hardware for a short time only.** It passed the host tests, the mutation tests
  of the changes and QEMU. On 2026-11-07 two controllers got the ESP32 image; their 13 valves got
  curves of up to 24 points (`[0,0]` and every 0.1 l/min from 0.5 to 2.7 l/min), one controller kept
  them over a restart, and every target in l/min went to the position of its own point, six valves at once,
  with the circulation pump running. The STM32 code is that of 2.2.9, which ran on both
  controllers before.
- **A curve of more than 16 points after a downgrade.** 2.2.14 and 2.2.13 show and run 16 of its
  points, without 0.5 l/min for the table above. A curve saved there replaces it; after a later
  2.2.15 a valve whose curve the older version did not change has all of its points again.
- **Event 419 is in the log only.** With the longest fields the event types of the Home Assistant
  event entity fill its payload, so `flow_curve_cut` is not published on MQTT. `curveCut` in
  `GET /api/valves/{n}/flow` shows the same.
- **`curveCut` can show on a whole curve.** After a failed removal of `flowpts`, or a save of
  2.2.14, a curve of exactly 16 points shows it too: the load cannot tell it from 16 points of a
  longer curve.
- **The flow is calculated, not measured.** The firmware has no flow sensor. The l/min it shows
  comes from the curve at the held position.
- **NC valve inserts only.** The motion rules were derived on normally closed inserts. Nobody
  tested normally open inserts.
